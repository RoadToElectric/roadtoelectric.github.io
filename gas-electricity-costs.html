How these numbers are built — read before quoting them
  1. The calculation. EV cost per 100 km = the car's consumption (kWh/100 km) × the home electricity price. Gas car cost per 100 km = its fuel use (L/100 km) × the gasoline price. Ratio = gas car cost ÷ EV cost: 3.0× means the EV costs a third as much per kilometre. $ saved in gas = (gas car cost − EV cost) × your yearly distance. A negative number (red) means charging at home costs more than filling up.
  2. Gasoline, Canada: Statistics Canada table 18-10-0001-01, regular unleaded at self-service stations, monthly, . StatCan publishes cities, not provinces, so each province is the plain average of its cities: Ontario = Toronto, Ottawa and Thunder Bay; Quebec = Montréal and Québec City; Alberta = Calgary and Edmonton; Saskatchewan = Regina and Saskatoon; British Columbia = Vancouver and Victoria; the others one city each; Yukon = Whitehorse, Northwest Territories = Yellowknife. Nunavut has no StatCan city and is left blank.
  3. Gasoline, United States: EIA monthly retail price of regular gasoline, taxes included, , for the 9 states with their own EIA series (California, Colorado, Florida, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New York, Ohio, Texas, Washington). The other 41 states: AAA's state averages (regular unleaded), read about twice a month over the same months ( readings, , from archived copies of AAA's state table), averaged, then put on EIA's level with one factor (×: AAA reads about 2.5% above EIA's survey). Checked on the states EIA publishes, the result lands within ±2.2% of EIA's own figure.
  4. Electricity, United States: EIA Electric Power Monthly, table 5.6.B, average residential price, January to August 2026. This is revenue ÷ kWh sold, so fixed monthly charges are spread over the kWh; it is a price before sales tax, which the page then adds (see below).
  5. Electricity, Canada: Canada Energy Regulator, market snapshot “How much do your neighbours across Canada pay for electricity?” (4 March 2026): modelled price for a home using 1,000 kWh a month, fixed and delivery charges and direct rebates included, taxes excluded, rates in force in November 2025 (Manitoba 1 January 2026, Nunavut 1 October 2022). Canada has no observed 2026 provincial average price, so this is the closest comparable measure.
  6. Sales tax on electricity is included. Both electricity sources give prices before tax, so the page adds the consumption tax a household pays on its electricity bill (the “Elec. tax” column; hover it for the detail), to compare with gasoline prices that already include their taxes. Canada (rules as of November 2026): GST 5% everywhere; Quebec + QST 9.975% = 14.975%; Ontario HST 13% (the Ontario Electricity Rebate is already in the CER price); Saskatchewan + PST 6% = 11%; Manitoba + RST 7% = 12% (the RST exemption covers space heating only, not EV charging); Newfoundland and Labrador HST 15%; Nova Scotia, New Brunswick and Prince Edward Island refund the provincial part of the HST on home electricity, so 5%; British Columbia, Alberta and the territories 5%. United States: the EIA price is revenue ÷ kWh as utilities report it (forms EIA-861 / 861M): it already includes the taxes and charges the utility bills itself (franchise fees, gross-receipts taxes, surcharges), but not the sales tax collected for the state. So the page adds the state sales tax on residential electricity, plus a typical local rate where local sales tax applies. About half the states exempt residential electricity (0%); some exempt part of it (Maine: the first 750 kWh a month; Minnesota and Wisconsin: winter heating only) or tax it at a reduced rate (Michigan 4%, Utah ~3%); Iowa and Kansas leave only the local tax. Rules checked against state law summaries (Bloomberg BNA utilities chart, state revenue departments). Not included: per-kWh excise taxes (Illinois, Virginia) and flat monthly or city utility taxes. Local rates vary by city, so a given address can differ by a point or two.
  7. Europe (42 countries): electricity = Eurostat household price (2,500–4,999 kWh a year band), first half of 2026, all taxes and levies already included, for the EU, Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, the Western Balkans, Moldova, Georgia and Türkiye; United Kingdom = Ofgem price cap Oct–Dec 2026 (26.32p/kWh + 54.83p/day standing charge over 2,700 kWh a year, electricity zero-rated for VAT in that period); Switzerland = ElCom 2026 median household tariff 27.7 Rp/kWh + 8.1% VAT; Ukraine = regulated tariff 4.32 UAH/kWh. Gasoline = European Commission Weekly Oil Bulletin, Euro-super 95 with all taxes, average of every week of 2026, for the 27 EU countries; outside the EU there is no official series: globalpetrolprices.com's Europe table (Octane-95) is read on every 2026 copy kept by the Internet Archive plus 9 November (8 readings, 29 Dec 2025 to 9 Nov 2026), each put on the Oil Bulletin's level that same week (factor from the EU countries both cover), and averaged as a level relative to the EU; tested on the 27 EU countries, this lands within 1% of the bulletin's 2026 average (4.2% at most). Georgia has one reading only. Liechtenstein uses the Swiss price; Kosovo has none. Rebuild: scripts/gas_elec/build_europe.py.
  8. Full cost comparison (purchase price, insurance, tires, oil changes, other yearly costs): see the EV vs ICE Costs Simulator.
  9. Home charging only: public fast charging often costs 2–4× the home price. Time-of-use or EV-specific rates can be much cheaper than the averages shown.
  10. EV consumption: every model sold in the US and Canada. The drop-down lists every battery-electric model rated by the US EPA (fueleconomy.gov, file of 4 November 2026; kWh per 100 miles converted to kWh/100 km) and by Natural Resources Canada (Fuel consumption ratings, 29 September 2026), model years 2024 to 2027, each model at its newest model year. A version NRCan also rates is marked as sold in both countries; 10 versions only NRCan rates are marked Canada only. Both agencies use the same 5-cycle test and measure energy from the wall, so charging losses are already included. The “Typical vehicles” at the top are rounded values for quick comparisons. Winter driving in Canada and the northern states can add 20–35%; use the Custom value to test it. Rebuild: scripts/gas_elec/build_ev_models.py.
  11. Currency: Canadian rows are in CAD, US rows in USD and European rows in EUR at the source; the page converts them with the Bank of Canada's 2026 average exchange rate ().
  12. Rebuild: scripts/gas_elec/build_gas_elec.py reads the source files kept in scripts/gas_elec/src/ and writes assets/gas-elec-data.js and the map shapes assets/na-admin1-geo.js (Natural Earth, public domain).
Data: Statistics Canada (table 18-10-0001-01), U.S. Energy Information Administration, AAA, Canada Energy Regulator, Bank of Canada; map: Natural Earth. Road to Electric — analyzing the current shift to EVs.
