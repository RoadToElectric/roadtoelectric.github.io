How these numbers are built — read before quoting them
  1. The calculation. EV cost per 100 km = the car's consumption (kWh/100 km) × the home electricity price. Gas car cost per 100 km = its fuel use (L/100 km) × the gasoline price. Ratio = gas car cost ÷ EV cost: 3.0× means the EV costs a third as much per kilometre. $ saved in gas = (gas car cost − EV cost) × your yearly distance. A negative number (red) means charging at home costs more than filling up.
  2. Gasoline, Canada: Statistics Canada table 18-10-0001-01, regular unleaded at self-service stations, monthly, . StatCan publishes cities, not provinces, so each province is the plain average of its cities: Ontario = Toronto, Ottawa and Thunder Bay; Quebec = Montréal and Québec City; Alberta = Calgary and Edmonton; Saskatchewan = Regina and Saskatoon; British Columbia = Vancouver and Victoria; the others one city each; Yukon = Whitehorse, Northwest Territories = Yellowknife. Nunavut has no StatCan city and is left blank.
  3. Gasoline, United States: EIA monthly retail price of regular gasoline, taxes included, . The EIA publishes its own series for only 9 states (California, Colorado, Florida, Massachusetts, Minnesota, New York, Ohio, Texas, Washington); those are used as is. The other 41 states are estimates (marked ≈): the 2026 average of the state's EIA region (New England, Central Atlantic, Lower Atlantic, Midwest, Gulf Coast, Rocky Mountain, West Coast) × how that state's AAA price compares with the AAA prices of the region's other states (AAA state averages, one-day reading of 14 November 2026). Tested on the 8 states EIA does publish, the estimate lands within ±6% of the real figure.
  4. Electricity, United States: EIA Electric Power Monthly, table 5.6.B, average residential price, January to August 2026. This is revenue ÷ kWh sold, so fixed monthly charges are spread over the kWh; it is a price before sales tax, which the page then adds (see below).
  5. Electricity, Canada: Canada Energy Regulator, market snapshot “How much do your neighbours across Canada pay for electricity?” (4 March 2026): modelled price for a home using 1,000 kWh a month, fixed and delivery charges and direct rebates included, taxes excluded, rates in force in November 2025 (Manitoba 1 January 2026, Nunavut 1 October 2022). Canada has no observed 2026 provincial average price, so this is the closest comparable measure.
  6. Sales tax on electricity is included. Both electricity sources give prices before tax, so the page adds the consumption tax a household pays on its electricity bill (the “Elec. tax” column; hover it for the detail), to compare with gasoline prices that already include their taxes. Canada (rules as of November 2026): GST 5% everywhere; Quebec + QST 9.975% = 14.975%; Ontario HST 13% (the Ontario Electricity Rebate is already in the CER price); Saskatchewan + PST 6% = 11%; Manitoba + RST 7% = 12% (the RST exemption covers space heating only, not EV charging); Newfoundland and Labrador HST 15%; Nova Scotia, New Brunswick and Prince Edward Island refund the provincial part of the HST on home electricity, so 5%; British Columbia, Alberta and the territories 5%. United States (approximate): state sales tax on residential electricity, or the state percentage tax charged on the bill in its place (Alabama utility tax 4%, Florida gross receipts 2.5%, Rhode Island 4%), plus a typical local rate where local sales tax applies. About half the states exempt residential electricity (0%). Not included: per-kWh excise taxes (Illinois, Virginia), flat monthly utility taxes, city utility taxes, and gross-receipts taxes the utility builds into its rates. Local rates vary by city, so a given address can differ by a few points.
  7. Cost comparator (section 2). Yearly cost = distance × consumption × price for each car, plus the optional items you tick: insurance (your two yearly amounts), tires (a set every tire life km for the gas car, a set every tire life ÷ (1 + extra wear %) km for the EV) and oil changes (one every x km for the gas car). Totals over 3, 5 and 10 years are simply the yearly cost multiplied by the number of years, at today's prices. Gas car consumption: EPA combined ratings of every gasoline and hybrid version of model years 2024–2027 (newest year per model); a version that needs premium fuel is marked, and its gas price should be raised (premium usually costs ~15% more). The default insurance, tire and oil figures are only starting points: enter your own.
  8. Home charging only: public fast charging often costs 2–4× the home price. Time-of-use or EV-specific rates can be much cheaper than the averages shown.
  9. EV consumption: every model sold in the US and Canada. The drop-down lists every battery-electric model rated by the US EPA (fueleconomy.gov, file of 4 November 2026; kWh per 100 miles converted to kWh/100 km) and by Natural Resources Canada (Fuel consumption ratings, 29 September 2026), model years 2024 to 2027, each model at its newest model year. A version NRCan also rates is marked as sold in both countries; 10 versions only NRCan rates are marked Canada only. Both agencies use the same 5-cycle test and measure energy from the wall, so charging losses are already included. The “Typical vehicles” at the top are rounded values for quick comparisons. Winter driving in Canada and the northern states can add 20–35%; use the Custom value to test it. Rebuild: scripts/gas_elec/build_ev_models.py.
  10. Currency: Canadian rows are in CAD and US rows in USD at the source; the page converts them with the Bank of Canada's 2026 average exchange rate ().
  11. Rebuild: scripts/gas_elec/build_gas_elec.py reads the source files kept in scripts/gas_elec/src/ and writes assets/gas-elec-data.js and the map shapes assets/na-admin1-geo.js (Natural Earth, public domain).
